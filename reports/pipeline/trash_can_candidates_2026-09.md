# Fact-checking the trash-can candidates

A document proposing an epistemic architecture for this project also supplied a list of "overlooked
and non-canonical" cognitive compounds with specific mechanisms, doses and claims. None of it could
enter a ledger unchecked. Five clusters were fact-checked against primary sources, claim by claim,
with instructions to refute rather than to confirm.

**Of 73 specific claims, 40 did not survive.** Exactly one cluster has genuine healthy-adult
cognitive evidence, and it belongs to a compound the document mentioned only in passing. None has
durable post-washout evidence in healthy adults.

This is not an argument against mining discards. It is what mining discards actually looks like when
the discrimination layer is applied, and three of the findings below are more useful to this project
than a confirmation would have been.

## The three findings worth the exercise

### Dihexa's foundational paper was retracted, and the mechanism points the wrong way

The paper establishing Dihexa's HGF/c-Met procognitive mechanism, Benoist et al. 2014 (PMID
25187433), was **RETRACTED on 29 April 2025** following an institutional investigation at Washington
State University that found image manipulation. Four papers from that laboratory received
Expressions of Concern from J Pharmacol Exp Ther in September 2021 (JPET 378(3):311-314), including
the paper underpinning the oral-activity and brain-penetrance claims (PMID 23055539).

The widely repeated "seven orders of magnitude more potent than BDNF" figure traces to a university
press release of 11 October 2012, not to a measurement. The ADDF Cognitive Vitality report states
that the two were never directly compared in the publication.

The decisive point for this project is neither of those. ADDF, verbatim: **"Dihexa did not improve
cognitive functions in rats with normal cognition."** The HGF/c-Met system appears to be an
injury-response mechanism that is not engaged during normal learning. A compound whose mechanism
only operates on a damaged substrate is not a healthy-adult enhancement candidate, whatever its
potency.

And the mechanism was not starved of a fair test. Dihexa's direct successor, fosgonimeton
(Athira Pharma, a WSU spin-off), ran five registered trials totalling roughly 1,090 participants.

### 4-MU dissociates the biomarker from the behaviour, on its own home assay

Dubisova et al. 2022 (PMID 35066096) is the perineuronal-net result the proposal rests on. Adult
mice on 5% w/w 4-MU chow, roughly 6.7 mg/g/day, for **six months**. Brain glycosaminoglycan fell to
72% of control and PNN staining decreased. Memory improved.

Then the washout arm, verbatim: "the memory-enhancing effect of 4-MU did not persist 1 month after
the end of the 4-MU treatment, and the SOR score returned to the values as before the treatment."
**The PNN reduction persisted. The memory effect did not.**

That is this project's binding constraint failing on the mechanism's own home assay, in the one
study that measured it: a structural plasticity marker that survives washout and a behavioural gain
that does not. Any pipeline that ranks compounds on predicted structural plasticity and infers
durable cognition is contradicted by its best available case.

The mechanism was also misdescribed. 4-MU does not degrade PNNs; it inhibits hyaluronan SYNTHESIS by
depleting UDP-glucuronic acid, so nets are not rebuilt. No study located measures 4-MU in brain
tissue or CSF at all, so CNS bioavailability sufficient for the effect is asserted rather than
established, and no registered hymecromone trial anywhere carries a cognitive or CNS endpoint.

### A flat misattribution, and two failures reported as successes

**TAK-071 is not an AMPA positive allosteric modulator.** It is a muscarinic M1 PAM, designed with
low cooperativity with acetylcholine to reduce cholinergic side effects, with no AMPA mechanism at
all (PMID 34240455, PMID 39761063). The proposal's "low-impact AMPA PAM" section is built on the
wrong molecule. Separately, the low-impact-versus-first-generation framing is a category error:
CX516 is both the first ampakine into human trials and the prototypical low-impact compound.

**Fosigotifator (ABBV-CLS-7262) and DNL343 both FAILED Phase 2/3 in ALS**, announced January 2025.
NCT05740813 (n = 310) missed its primary endpoint at both dose levels; NCT05842941 (n = 249) missed
its primary endpoint with neurofilament light unchanged. The eIF2B-activator clinical programme has
two adequately powered negatives, not the successes implied.

## Cluster by cluster

### Klotho: real and interesting, three claims misdescribed, zero human exposure

Castner et al., Nature Aging 2023 (PMID 37400721) is real. A single subcutaneous 10 ug/kg dose in
aged rhesus macaques improved spatial delayed response, and the benefit was still present at 14 to
23 days (P = 0.0035). Persistence for weeks after one dose is the most interesting durability signal
in the entire list.

Three claims do not survive. The **bell-shaped dose response is the authors' interpretation, not a
demonstrated finding**: the 20 and 30 ug/kg arms are labelled exploratory, animals were not
randomised to dose, no dose-by-response interaction statistic appears anywhere, and the same paper
notes that mice "continued to show cognitive enhancement with much higher KL doses." The
**post-washout framing overstates what was done**: the primary 4-hour timepoint is on-drug, and no
serum klotho was measured at the durable timepoint. The **GluN2B-into-the-postsynaptic-density claim
is imported from the wrong paradigm**: true for lifelong transgenic overexpression (PMID 24813892),
explicitly contradicted in the acute peripheral-protein paradigm the macaque protocol actually
models (Leon 2017, PMID 28793260, where synaptic GluN2B did not differ despite cognitive gain).

No human has received recombinant alpha-klotho in any registered or published study. The lead
candidate has an IND targeted for Q4 2026.

Note also that the analysis unit is SESSIONS, not animals, so no per-animal effect size with an
interval is recoverable.

### PDE4: the one genuine healthy-adult asset, and it is roflumilast

Zatolmilast (BPN14770) has no healthy-adult cognitive efficacy data. Rolipram has none. The asset
belongs to **low-dose roflumilast at 100 ug**, with two real healthy-adult crossovers: PMID 29241652
(healthy young adults, immediate recall on a 30-word verbal learning task improved by 2 to 3 words
with an enhanced P600) and PMID 30776650 (healthy adults aged 60 to 80, n = 20, delayed recall,
Cohen's d = 0.69).

Three caveats decide how it should be used. The two studies **disagree about which endpoint moves**:
immediate recall in the young, delayed recall in the old, spatial memory in neither. That is the G3
assay-dependence failure mode arising inside a single drug and a single research group. Both come
from the same Maastricht group with industry co-authors and are not independent replications. A
third healthy-adult RCT with posted results, NCT02051335 (n = 27), found roflumilast monotherapy did
NOT significantly improve verbal recall. And the only well-powered test of the 100 ug dose,
ROSTMEMA (PMID 42032844, n = 100), MISSED its pre-specified primary analysis and became significant
only after post hoc covariate adjustment.

Every healthy-adult study in this cluster is a single acute dose about an hour before testing. There
is no durability evidence.

### NSI-189: two adequately powered failures under two sponsors

The Phase 2 "cognitive improvement" was on **self-report instruments at 40 mg only** (SDQ -8.2,
p = 0.04; MGH-CPFQ -1.9, p = 0.035), while the **objective Cogstate battery showed no significant
differences at either dose**, and all measures were null at 80 mg. Calling that cognitive improvement
conflates a cognitive-complaint questionnaire in depressed patients with objective performance.

The trial had **no washout or follow-up period at all**, so the claimed post-washout persistence is
asserting something the trial did not measure.

The molecule is now ALTO-100 (amdiglurax). Alto's Phase 2b (NCT05712187, n = 301) selected patients
on an objective memory-linked biomarker, enriching for exactly the population the mechanism
predicts, and still missed its primary endpoint in October 2024.

The single healthy-volunteer study, NCT01310881, measured adverse events and pharmacokinetics. No
cognitive battery was administered.

### Tropisetron: the alpha7 attribution does not hold

The binding Ki at alpha7 is 6.9 nM, but the functional partial-agonist EC50 is about 2.4 uM (PMID
28259598), roughly 350-fold higher, so nanomolar affinity does not imply target engagement at
ordinary clinical doses. "Selective" in the source means selective among nicotinic subtypes, not
over 5-HT3: the reported affinities are 5-HT3 Ki 5.3 nM versus alpha7 Ki 6.9 nM, marginally favouring
5-HT3. No human endpoint can be attributed to alpha7 on affinity grounds.

A systematic review of exactly this question (PMID 35017671) found 11 studies, of which the three in
healthy volunteers used granisetron and ondansetron, not tropisetron, and returned null or negative
results. There is no healthy-adult cognitive or P50 data for tropisetron.

### ISRIB: the toxicity claim is a misreading, and a compound mix-up

"Paradoxical toxicity at higher doses" misreads Rabouw 2019 (PMID 30674674), which describes a
narrow window of STRESS ACTIVATION, not of drug dose, and frames it as the opposite of a toxicity
finding. Halliday 2015 (PMID 25741597) reports no dose-limiting in vivo toxicity and increased
lifespan in prion-diseased mice.

The canine cardiovascular liability belongs to **2BAct**, Calico's analogue, not to ISRIB. That is a
compound mix-up, and the kind that propagates.

ISRIB has zero human exposure of any kind. The shape of the absence is the useful part: in two cases
in this cluster healthy adults HAVE been dosed and nobody measured cognition.

## What was done with this

The proposal's compounds produced no ledger rows. Low-dose roflumilast is the only candidate with
genuine healthy-adult evidence, and it is acute, contested by a third trial, internally inconsistent
about its endpoint, and therefore a curation task rather than a finding.

The three structural results above were more valuable than any candidate would have been: a
retracted evidence base behind a widely promoted compound, a measured dissociation between a
structural plasticity biomarker and durable behaviour, and a mechanism misattributed to the wrong
receptor class. All three are the discrimination layer working as intended, and all three are
recorded in `data/raw/stepping_stone_archive.csv` so that a later capability can reopen them if one
ever should.
