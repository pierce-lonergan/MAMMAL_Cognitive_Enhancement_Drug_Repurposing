# B4 — The N* gate: what a replication of the one existence proof actually costs

Reproduced by `scripts/126_replication_power.py`. Pre-registered: SUCCESS if N* <= 60/arm under the shrunk effect; KILL if N* > 250/arm.

## VERDICT: **AMBIGUOUS** — 104 per arm on the conventional planning value

## Deriving the effect size from what Rokem & Silver actually reported

Reported: percent learning **47.1 ± 4.6** (donepezil) vs **34.2 ± 6.9** (placebo), SEM, n=8 returners.

- SD = SEM x sqrt(n): drug **13.01**, placebo **19.52**
- pooled SD **16.59**, mean difference **12.9**
- **between-group d = 0.778**

## N* per arm (two-sample, two-sided, alpha=0.05, 80% power)

| planning assumption | d | N per arm | total N |
|---|---|---|---|
| observed (n=8, winner's-curse inflated) | 0.778 | **26** | 52 |
| 50% shrunk (conventional planning value) | 0.389 | **104** | 208 |
| meta-analytic augmentation ceiling | 0.250 | **252** | 504 |
| project acute ceiling (nicotine, best case) | 0.340 | **136** | 272 |

**Why the shrunk row is the one to plan on.** A significant result at n=8 is subject to the winner's curse: conditional on clearing p<0.05 at that size, the observed effect is an upward-biased estimate of the truth. Rokem & Silver compounds this — the surviving advantage is in percent learning *normalised to each arm's own pre-training baseline*, and the paper reports a near-significant baseline imbalance (rank p=0.05, one subject at z=2.42) that mechanically inflates exactly that quantity. Planning on d=0.78 would be planning on the most fragile number in the literature.

## The confound that makes cheap designs uninterpretable

The canonical practice (retest) effect on cognitive composites with **no intervention at all** is d ~ 0.26. The meta-analytic ceiling for drug x training augmentation is d ~ 0.25. So:

> **artifact / target = 1.04** — the noise a design must subtract is *larger than the signal it is looking for*.

Consequences for the design, not negotiable:
- a **pre-randomisation repeated-testing burn-in** to saturate practice gains before the intervention starts;
- **between-group** allocation, not the cheaper within-subject crossover — the one drug x training crossover in the literature died of carryover (significant Condition x Order interaction) and its blind was penetrated 17 of 18 participants;
- the **retained off-drug LEVEL** as primary endpoint, never the learning curve: the one trial that scored the curve bought a rate effect (AUC ES 0.84) that placebo erased by the final session, with zero transfer.

## What the money actually buys

If the true effect is the meta-analytic augmentation ceiling, a decisive trial is **252 per arm = 504 participants**, each requiring a training course plus a >=1-month off-drug retest — to durably improve performance on **one motion-discrimination task**, with **no evidence of transfer** to any other cognitive measure. That is the honest price, and it belongs on the record before anyone commits to it.

Pre-registered thresholds: SUCCESS <= 60/arm, KILL > 250/arm. The shrunk planning value lands at **104/arm**, and the pessimistic-but-defensible meta-analytic value at **252/arm** — i.e. the outcome straddles the thresholds: reachable only if the true effect is at least half the inflated n=8 estimate, and out of single-site reach if it is the augmentation ceiling.

## Bottom line

The replication is **not impossible, but it is not cheap, and it is not a cognitive-enhancement trial** — it is a trial of whether a drug amplifies retention of one trained perceptual skill. B1 independently found that the drug x experience contrast reverses sign once studies with n >= 25 are required, so the prior going into such a trial should be low.
